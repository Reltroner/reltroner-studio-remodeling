## 🌕 Asthortera's 12 Orbiting Moons and Calendar System

Asthortera is orbited by **12 natural satellites**, forming the symbolic foundation of its **12-month, 365-day calendar system**. These moons are categorized into **4 major moons** (Earth-sized) and **8 minor moons** (dwarf satellites). Each month in the Asthorteran calendar is named after one of these moons.

Each moon of Asthortera is not merely a celestial object. Each is a **symbolic pillar in the sentient rhythm of time**, shaping calendars, rituals, cultural memory, emotional symbolism, astronomical observation, and the philosophies of civilizations across the planet.

However, the existence of twelve moons should not be interpreted to mean that all twelve moons are visible from every location at every moment.

The **Twelve Moons are permanent astronomical bodies, but their visibility is highly variable**. At a given location and time, observers may see **zero, one, several, or—during exceptional astronomical circumstances—many of the twelve moons**.

Their apparent visibility depends on several interacting factors:

* orbital position;
* orbital period;
* orbital inclination and geometry;
* the moon's position relative to the local horizon;
* illumination and lunar phase;
* atmospheric clarity;
* observational conditions;
* latitude and geographic location;
* daylight or nighttime conditions;
* distance and apparent brightness;
* and temporary astronomical alignments.

Therefore:

> **Twelve moons exist continuously. Twelve moons are not continuously visible.**

This distinction is an important part of Asthortera's astronomical canon and applies to both scientific observation and visual representations of the planet.

# 🌌 The Asthorteran Lunar Visibility Principle

The official astronomical principle governing the Twelve Moons is:

> **The Twelve Moons are permanent astronomical bodies, but their visibility is highly variable. A given location may observe anywhere from zero to several moons at a particular time. Their apparent number depends on orbital position, horizon geometry, illumination, atmospheric conditions, geographic location, and observational clarity.**

This means that a sky over Asthortera may legitimately contain:

* no visible moons;
* one visible moon;
* two or several visible moons;
* multiple moons at different apparent brightnesses;
* a prominent moon accompanied by faint secondary moons;
* or, during rare astronomical circumstances, a large number of simultaneously visible moons.

The **absence of a visible moon is therefore not evidence that Asthortera has fewer than twelve moons**.

A moon may be physically present in the Asthorteran planetary system while remaining invisible to a particular observer because it is:

1. below the local horizon;
2. positioned on the opposite side of Asthortera;
3. insufficiently illuminated;
4. too close to the Sun's apparent position in the sky;
5. too faint under the prevailing atmospheric conditions;
6. outside the observer's optimal viewing window;
7. obscured by atmospheric phenomena;
8. or temporarily hidden by the geometry of its orbit.

Consequently, **moon presence is not mandatory in every Asthorteran landscape, cityscape, airport scene, architectural scene, or cinematic composition**.

## 🌗 Moon Presence in Visual Worldbuilding

Asthortera's twelve moons are an important visual identity of the planet, but they are **not mandatory visual objects in every depiction**.

A scene should not automatically be considered non-canonical merely because no moon appears in the sky.

The following visual states are all canonically valid:

### 🌑 Zero Visible Moons

A scene may show a completely moonless sky.

Possible explanations include:

* all moons are below the local horizon;
* several moons are on the opposite side of the planet;
* visible moons are in poorly illuminated phases;
* atmospheric conditions suppress faint lunar bodies;
* the scene occurs during a natural moonless interval;
* or the scene occurs during a formally recognized **Voidnight**.

Therefore:

> **A moonless Asthorteran sky is canonically valid.**

---

### 🌒 One Visible Moon

A scene may show only one moon.

The other eleven moons may simultaneously exist but remain observationally unavailable because of orbital geometry, phase, horizon position, brightness, or atmospheric conditions.

A single visible moon should therefore not automatically be interpreted as implying that Asthortera has only one moon.

---

### 🌓 Several Visible Moons

Scenes may show two, three, or several moons.

The apparent brightness, size, and position of each moon may vary substantially.

Not every visible moon needs to appear equally large or equally bright.

---

### 🌕 Exceptional Multi-Moon Skies

Rare astronomical circumstances may produce skies containing many simultaneously visible moons.

Such scenes should generally be treated as **astronomically significant moments**, rather than the default appearance of Asthortera.

Examples include:

* major orbital alignments;
* ceremonial astronomical events;
* unusual seasonal configurations;
* planetary observatory events;
* major spiritual festivals;
* or scientifically significant conjunctions.

A sky containing many visible moons can therefore be used intentionally when the story calls attention to Asthortera's unusual celestial architecture.

# 🌑 Voidnight

**Voidnight** is the formal Asthorteran term for a naturally occurring period in which **none of the twelve moons are visibly observable from a particular region or observational reference point**.

Voidnight may occur when multiple lunar visibility factors overlap, including:

* unfavorable orbital positions;
* moons below the horizon;
* insufficient illumination;
* lunar phases that produce minimal apparent brightness;
* atmospheric interference;
* local geographical conditions;
* and temporary orbital or magnetic configurations.

Voidnight therefore represents the extreme end of Asthortera's lunar visibility spectrum.

It is not the absence of the moons themselves.

The Twelve Moons continue to exist and orbit Asthortera during Voidnight.

Only their **observability from the relevant location** reaches zero.

* not everything that exists must be visible;

Voidnights are therefore associated with:

* introspection;

* silence;
* philosophical reflection;
* observatory vigils;
* spiritual contemplation;

# 🌌 Interpreting Lunar Visibility

The Moon Registry should not be interpreted as a deterministic daily visibility calendar.

For any specific observer on Asthortera, the visible lunar population at a particular moment is determined by the intersection of:

**Orbital Position**

→ where the moon is in its orbit

**Planetary Geometry**

→ where the moon is relative to Asthortera and the observer

**Horizon Position**

→ whether the moon is above or below the local horizon

**Illumination**

→ how much reflected light reaches the observer

**Atmospheric Conditions**

→ how clearly the moon can be observed

**Geographic Location**

→ latitude, longitude, elevation, and local horizon

**Observation Time**

→ local time and seasonal conditions

Therefore:

> **Visibility frequency is statistical, not deterministic.**

Two cities on Asthortera may observe different numbers of moons at the same moment.

Likewise, two observers in the same city may see different apparent lunar populations because of buildings, mountains, weather, atmospheric conditions, or their specific viewing direction.

# 🌙 Lunar Visibility States

For worldbuilding and visual production purposes, Asthorteran skies can be classified into five broad observational states.

### State I — Void Sky

**Zero visible moons.**

All twelve satellites remain physically present within the planetary system, but none are observationally available from the relevant location.

This includes formal Voidnight conditions.

---

### State II — Sparse Sky

**One visible moon.**

This is an entirely normal observational state.

The other moons remain hidden because of their orbital positions, phases, horizon positions, or other visibility conditions.

---

### State III — Layered Sky

**Two to several visible moons.**

Different moons may appear at different heights, phases, brightnesses, and apparent sizes.

This is one of the most visually distinctive expressions of Asthortera's multi-moon environment.

---

### State IV — Convergent Sky

**Many visible moons.**

This is an uncommon astronomical configuration in which several satellites become simultaneously observable.

Such events may attract scientific, cultural, or spiritual attention.

---

### State V — Celestial Convergence

**An exceptional multi-moon astronomical event.**

A large portion of the Twelve Moons becomes visible simultaneously under highly favorable orbital and observational conditions.

These events are rare and may be recorded by observatories, celebrated by civilizations, or incorporated into major historical events.

# 🎨 Visual Canon Rule for Asthortera

The following rule is now considered part of the official visual canon:

> **Asthortera's twelve moons should be treated as a persistent astronomical system, not as mandatory background objects in every visual representation.**

When generating or evaluating artwork:

### Moonless scenes are valid.

A scene does **not** need a visible moon simply because it takes place on Asthortera.

### Single-moon scenes are valid.

One visible moon does not imply the absence of the other eleven.

### Multi-moon scenes are valid.

Several visible moons may be used when the scene benefits from a stronger expression of Asthortera's celestial identity.

### Twelve-moon scenes should be intentional.

Displaying all twelve moons simultaneously should generally communicate an unusual astronomical event rather than serve as the default background for ordinary scenes.

### Lunar visibility should support the scene.

The number of visible moons should be determined by:

* time of day;
* location;
* narrative context;
* astronomical circumstances;
* visual composition;
* and the intended emotional atmosphere.

The purpose of the rule is to prevent the Twelve Moons from becoming a forced visual checklist.
